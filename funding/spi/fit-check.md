# SPI fit check

**Status:** research and unanswered questions, not an eligibility determination.

**Sources checked:** October 17, 2026.

## What SPI publishes

- Its intake process begins with informal discussions. Projects should be genuine, substantial, and significant free/open-source projects; acceptance is not automatic.
- A formal application identifies the project, services needed, a liaison, and a process for replacing that liaison. Existing contracts, liabilities, and pending litigation must be disclosed where applicable.
- SPI can accept project donations and pay or reimburse documented expenses that further its goals. Contracts require appropriate SPI authorization.
- Its published overhead is 5% of donated amounts, with attributable transaction costs deducted separately.
- Donor tax treatment depends on the applicable tax system. U.S. charitable status does not establish deductions worldwide.

These published services do not establish approval for OogaBoogaX, any specific AI vendor, or a particular subscription arrangement.

## Other routes to revisit if needed

- [Software Freedom Conservancy](https://sfconservancy.org/projects/apply/): selective admission, recognized licensing requirements, and a preference for established projects. Its guidance says projects under a year old are generally not eligible.
- [OpenSats](https://opensats.org/apply): grants for qualifying Bitcoin-related public work. Its RED Token Reimbursement program concerns LLM/compute use for security research; it does not establish support for all development subscriptions. This is a grant route, not a confirmed offer of fiscal sponsorship.
- Independent nonprofit formation: assess actual program eligibility, governance capacity, jurisdiction, and expected gross receipts before choosing an application route.
