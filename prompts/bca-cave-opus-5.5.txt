10. VISIBLE COMPUTER AND ABACUS WORK

Create a calm, staggered work loop. Suggested pacing is illustrative; tune it to the existing animation style:

  - Type for approximately 5–9 seconds: small alternating hand/wrist motions,
    restrained shoulder movement, and gaze directed toward the computer.
  - Pause and inspect the screen for approximately 1–2 seconds.
  - Shift attention to the abacus, reach toward a row, and calculate for roughly
    3–5 seconds: move a small bead group along a rod, pause, and withdraw the hand.
  - Glance back at the monitor or ledger, then resume computer work.

Vary the timing deterministically per gorilla and station so the two accountants do not perform the same motion in lockstep. Keep most movement small and focused. A rare thoughtful head tilt or receipt check is enough personality; avoid incessant chest beating, frantic waving, or exaggerated comedy loops.

Abacus use must be physically readable: the hand reaches the actual frame/row, the beads visibly travel along their rods, groups stay separated rather than intersecting, and the hand withdraws. Animate a bounded set of existing bead nodes or instances. Do not create/delete beads during every calculation, and do not treat every bead as an independent physics obstacle.

Keyboard motion must meet the keyboard. Abacus motion must meet the abacus. Derive targets from the same workstation layout that places the geometry. Avoid decorative hand waving in empty air.
